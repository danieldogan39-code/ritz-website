High currents. Precisely transformed.

An instrument transformer converts currents and voltages from the grid into small, standardised measuring signals – proportional, in phase and galvanically isolated. Meters, protection relays and grid control rely on it, often for decades.

Growing since 1945

From its founding in Hamburg to the new high-voltage plant in the USA: eight decades of instrument transformer engineering.

Company history
  1. 1945FoundationDr Hans Ritz founds RITZ Messwandler in Hamburg.
  2. 1956AustriaRITZ is established in Marchtrenk.
  3. 1991TuR DresdenAcquisition of the instrument transformer division of Transformatoren- und Röntgenwerk Dresden; high-voltage production in Waynesboro.
  4. 2003ShanghaiRITZ Shanghai produces in its own factory.
  5. 2008RITZ USARITZ is established in Hartwell (USA) with medium- and low-voltage transformers.
  6. 2026New high-voltage plantOpening of the production and test centre for high-voltage transformers in Waynesboro, Georgia (USA).

Every device tested.

Every unit is routine-tested. Our test bays work with AC voltages up to 400 kV and currents up to 50 kA; the Dresden plant houses a state-recognised testing authority for official verification.
